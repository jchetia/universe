01 — THE COSMIC HIERARCHY

Everything Is Connected

Follow the structure of the cosmos, from the planet beneath our feet to the largest observable structures.

01

🌍 Earth

OUR HOME

Meaning: Earth is the planet where we live. It contains oceans, continents, an atmosphere, and all currently known life.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun. It rotates on its axis and revolves around the Sun.

Includes: Continents, oceans, countries, ecosystems, and living organisms.
02

☀️ Solar System

STARS & PLANETS

Meaning: A planetary system consists of a star and objects gravitationally bound to it, including planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets.

Our Solar System has the Sun and eight planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

Structure: The Sun, planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets.
03

🌌 Galaxy

BILLIONS OF STARS

Meaning: A galaxy is a huge system of stars, stellar remnants, gas, dust, and dark matter held together by gravity.

Our Solar System is located inside the Milky Way, which contains hundreds of billions of stars.

Examples: Milky Way, Andromeda Galaxy, and Triangulum Galaxy.
04

✨ Galaxy Group

GALAXIES TOGETHER

Meaning: A galaxy group is a collection of galaxies held together by gravity. Groups generally contain fewer galaxies than rich galaxy clusters.

Our Milky Way belongs to the Local Group, alongside Andromeda, Triangulum, and many smaller galaxies.

Example: The Local Group contains dozens of known galaxies.
05

🌠 Galaxy Cluster

LARGE GALAXY SYSTEMS

Meaning: A galaxy cluster is a large gravitationally bound structure containing many galaxies, hot gas, and dark matter.

Rich clusters can contain hundreds or thousands of galaxies.

Examples: Virgo Cluster and Coma Cluster.
06

🔮 Galaxy Supercluster

COSMIC MEGASTRUCTURES

Meaning: A supercluster is an enormous region containing galaxy groups and clusters spread across vast distances.

Our Local Group lies within the Laniakea Supercluster region. A supercluster is not necessarily gravitationally bound as a whole.

Example: Laniakea Supercluster.
07

🕸️ Cosmic Web

LARGE-SCALE STRUCTURE

Meaning: The cosmic web is an enormous network of galaxy filaments, sheets, dense regions, and vast low-density areas called cosmic voids.

Gravity helps matter gather along filaments. Galaxies and clusters occupy parts of this network, while voids contain comparatively little matter.

Structure: Filaments, sheets, dense nodes, and cosmic voids.
08

🌌 Observable Universe

OUR COSMIC HORIZON

Meaning: The observable universe is the region from which light or other signals have had time to reach us since the universe began evolving about 13.8 billion years ago.

Because space expanded while that light travelled, the observable universe is approximately 93 billion light-years across.

Includes all observable cosmic structures within our horizon. The entire universe may extend far beyond it.

🌌 Universe — Endless Mystery

THE GRAND COSMOS

Meaning: The universe includes all space, time, matter, energy, and the physical laws that govern everything that exists.

Its complete size and ultimate boundaries remain unknown. We can observe only a limited region of the universe, and we do not know whether the entire cosmos is infinite or finite.

Scientists also do not know exactly how the universe will end. It may continue expanding forever, gradually becoming colder and darker as stars stop forming and existing stars fade away. The ultimate fate of the cosmos remains one of science's greatest unanswered questions.

The Greatest Cosmic Mystery: Does the universe have an end? What lies beyond what we can observe? And what will happen in the distant future of the cosmos? These questions remain open for scientific exploration.
02 — EXPLORE THE COSMOS

Discover the Universe

Explore fascinating celestial objects, distant galaxies, mysterious black holes, and humanity's journey into deep space.

EXTREME GRAVITY

Black Hole TON 618

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape from within its event horizon. TON 618 is an extremely distant and luminous quasar powered by a supermassive black hole, with an estimated mass of around 40 billion times that of the Sun, making it one of the most massive known black holes in the observable universe. Its event horizon has an estimated diameter of approximately 0.025 light-years (240 billion kilometres), making it vastly larger than our entire Solar System.

SPACE ROCKS

Asteroids

Asteroids are rocky objects that orbit the Sun. Many are found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

ICY TRAVELLERS

Comets

Comets are icy bodies that orbit the Sun. Solar heating can create a glowing coma and spectacular tails.

INTERSTELLAR EXPLORATION

Voyager 1

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 is a NASA spacecraft that entered interstellar space in 2012 and continues its journey beyond our solar system.

EARTH'S NATURAL SATELLITE

The Moon

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. Its gravitational influence helps produce ocean tides.

OUR HOME STAR

The Sun

The Sun is the star at the center of our Solar System. Its energy supports life on Earth, and its gravity keeps planets in orbit.

OUR NEAREST STELLAR NEIGHBOR

Proxima Centauri

Proxima Centauri is the closest known star to the Sun, located about 4.24 light-years away. It is a small red dwarf star and belongs to the Alpha Centauri star system.

OUR GALACTIC NEIGHBOR

Andromeda Galaxy

The Andromeda Galaxy is a large spiral galaxy about 2.5 million light-years from Earth. It is the nearest major galaxy to the Milky Way, and the two galaxies are moving toward each other.

EXTREME STELLAR SIZE

Stephenson 2-18

Stephenson 2-18 is a red supergiant candidate often described as one of the largest known stars by estimated radius. Its enormous size remains uncertain because stellar measurements are difficult. It is estimated to be so enormous that about 10 billion Suns could fit inside it by volume.
